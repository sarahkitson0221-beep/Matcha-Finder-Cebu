    Matcha vs green tea

    Both come from the same plant, Camellia sinensis. The difference is how the leaf is grown and prepared.

    MatchaShade-grown leaves, stone-ground into a fine powder and whisked into water. The whole leaf is consumed.
    Green teaLeaves steeped in hot water, then removed. Only what dissolves into the water is consumed.

    Matcha grades

    Grades describe how the tea is meant to be used. There is no single official standard, so labels vary by brand.

    CeremonialYoungest leaves, finest grind. Made to drink with water only.
    Premium / latteBalanced flavor, often used with milk.
    CulinaryStronger and more bitter. Used in baking, cooking and blended drinks.
    How matcha is labeled

    Usucha & koicha

    Usucha (thin tea): about 2 g of matcha whisked with around 70 ml of water until frothy.

    Koicha (thick tea): roughly double the matcha with less water, kneaded slowly instead of whisked. It is usually made with higher grades.

    3 matcha myths

    1. It has more caffeine than coffee.A typical serving has less caffeine than a cup of brewed coffee.
    2. It's always bitter.Bitterness increases with water temperature. Around 70–80°C is commonly recommended.
    3. It needs special equipment.A bamboo whisk is traditional, but a frother or a shaken jar also mixes the powder.
